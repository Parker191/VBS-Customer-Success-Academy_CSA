# 📋 QBR Follow-Through & Action Management Workflow

## Purpose

Provide a structured Customer Success workflow for managing actions, decisions, commitments, and follow-through after a Quarterly Business Review (QBR).

The objective is to ensure that the QBR does not end with the meeting.

The CSM should convert the QBR discussion into:

- Clear decisions
- Confirmed actions
- Assigned owners
- Agreed deadlines
- Customer commitments
- Internal commitments
- Risk mitigation
- Future priorities
- CRM updates
- Ongoing follow-through

A successful QBR is not measured only by how well the meeting went.

It is measured by what happens after the meeting.

1️⃣ Review Meeting Notes

Immediately after the QBR, review the meeting notes.

Capture:

Decisions
Commitments
Open questions
Risks
Concerns
Future priorities
Potential opportunities
Customer requests

Do not rely only on memory.

Use the meeting notes, transcript, or confirmed discussion record.

2️⃣ Validate Decisions

Identify decisions that were actually agreed upon.

Examples:

Weekly reporting will continue.
A delivery review will be conducted.
Customer will provide upcoming project requirements.
VBS will review capacity planning.
Another stakeholder will be included in future meetings.

Do not convert suggestions into decisions.

[Decision]	[Meeting discussion]	[Person]	[Date]

If a decision was discussed but not finalized:

3️⃣ Extract Action Items

Convert confirmed commitments into actionable tasks.

Create:

Action	Owner	Deadline	Priority	Status
[Action]	[Owner]	[Date]	[Priority]	Open

Only create an action when there is sufficient evidence that the action was agreed upon.

If ownership is unclear:

Owner: To be Confirmed

If the deadline is unclear:

Deadline: To be Confirmed

Do not invent either.

6️⃣ Separate Customer & VBS Actions

7️⃣ Identify Risks / Blockers

Review all actions and identify anything that could prevent successful follow-through.

Potential blockers:

Missing customer information
Internal resource constraints
Delivery dependencies
Delayed approvals
Unclear requirements
Stakeholder availability
Technical limitations
Commercial approval requirements

Create:

Risk / Blocker	Evidence	Impact	Mitigation	Owner
[Risk]	[Evidence]	[Impact]	[Action]	[Owner]

Do not classify something as a risk without supporting evidence.

8️⃣ Prioritize Actions

Not every action has equal importance.

Use:

🔴 High Priority

Customer-impacting or time-sensitive actions.

🟡 Medium Priority

Important actions that support the relationship or future planning.

🟢 Low Priority

Useful improvements that do not require immediate attention.

Prioritize based on:

Customer impact
Business impact
Urgency
Risk
Dependency

1️⃣3️⃣ Follow Up on Overdue Actions

If an important action becomes overdue:

Identify the reason.
Contact the owner.
Understand the blocker.
Determine a revised timeline if appropriate.
Update the customer when relevant.
Escalate if customer impact is significant.

Do not silently allow customer-impacting actions to remain overdue.

1️⃣4️⃣ Validate Customer Impact

Completion of an action does not automatically mean success.

Ask:

Did the action actually improve the customer's situation?

Examples:

Action

Improve delivery visibility.

Completion

Weekly status report implemented.

Customer Impact

Customer confirms that delivery visibility has improved.

The CSM should track both:

Action Completion

and

Customer Impact

1️⃣5️⃣ Update Customer Health

After significant QBR follow-through, reassess customer health.

Review:

Customer sentiment
Delivery performance
Engagement
Relationship
Open issues
Risk level
Customer feedback
Value delivered

Health may:

🟢 Improve

🟡 Remain Stable

🟠 Deteriorate

🔴 Become Critical

Do not change health simply because an action was completed.

Use evidence.

1️⃣6️⃣ Capture Lessons Learned

After completing the QBR cycle, document:

What worked?

[What was successful]

What did not work?

[What could be improved]

What did we learn?

[Learning]

What should change next time?

[Improvement]

Use these insights to improve future QBRs.

1️⃣7️⃣ Prepare for Next Review

Before the next customer review, revisit:

Previous QBR commitments
Action completion
Customer impact
Customer feedback
Risks
Future priorities
Value delivered
New requirements

The previous QBR should become input for the next customer conversation.

📈 QBR Follow-Through Status

The CSM can classify follow-through as:

🟢 On Track

Actions are progressing according to agreed timelines.

🟡 Needs Attention

Some actions require follow-up or clarification.

🟠 At Risk

Important actions are delayed or blocked.

🔴 Critical

Customer-impacting commitments are significantly delayed or unresolved.

⚪ Unknown

There is insufficient information to determine status.

Do not classify based on assumptions.

⚠️ Important Rules

AI assists — CSM decides.

Do not:

Invent action items.
Invent owners.
Invent deadlines.
Treat suggestions as commitments.
Mark actions complete without evidence.
Assume customer impact.
Hide overdue actions.
Promise unapproved solutions.
Create unauthorized commercial commitments.
Change customer health without evidence.
Treat a completed internal task as proof of customer success.

The CSM remains responsible for validating all important information.

🎯 Desired Outcome

The workflow should help the CSM move from:

QBR → Decisions → Actions → Follow-Through → Customer Impact → Continuous Improvement

The ultimate goal is to ensure that the QBR creates measurable customer value and that agreed actions do not get lost after the meeting.
