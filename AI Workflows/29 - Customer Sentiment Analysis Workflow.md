# ❤️ Customer Sentiment Analysis Workflow

## Purpose

Provide a structured AI-assisted workflow for analyzing customer communication and identifying sentiment, concerns, positive signals, changes in tone, and potential Customer Success actions.

The objective is to help CSMs recognize customer sentiment signals while avoiding unsupported assumptions.

---

# 1️⃣ Collect Relevant Communication

Review relevant customer communication.

Possible sources:

- Emails
- Meeting notes
- Call notes
- Customer feedback
- Support discussions
- Escalation messages
- Survey responses
- QBR / MBR feedback
- Project updates

Use recent and relevant communication.

Avoid analyzing unrelated information.

---

# 2️⃣ Identify Customer Statements

Separate actual customer statements from:

- Internal comments
- CSM assumptions
- Team interpretations
- AI-generated conclusions

Focus on what the customer actually communicated.

Example:

**Customer Statement:**

> "We are concerned about the delivery timeline."

This is evidence.

---

# 3️⃣ Analyze Sentiment

Classify sentiment using:

🟢 **Positive**

Customer expresses satisfaction, appreciation, confidence, or positive experience.

🔵 **Neutral**

Customer provides information without clear positive or negative emotion.

🟡 **Mixed**

Customer expresses both positive and negative signals.

🟠 **Negative**

Customer expresses dissatisfaction, frustration, concern, or disappointment.

🔴 **Critical**

Customer expresses severe dissatisfaction, loss of confidence, escalation, or potential relationship/renewal risk.

⚪ **Unknown**

There is not enough information to determine sentiment.

---

# 4️⃣ Identify Positive Signals

Look for signals such as:

- Appreciation
- Satisfaction
- Confidence
- Positive feedback
- Recognition of good work
- Successful delivery
- Positive relationship comments
- Willingness to continue
- Interest in future work
- Referral or advocacy signals

Create:

| Positive Signal | Evidence | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| [Signal] | [Customer statement] | [High/Medium/Low] |

Do not label a customer as fully healthy based on one positive comment.

---

# 5️⃣ Identify Negative Signals

Look for:

- Complaints
- Frustration
- Delays
- Repeated concerns
- Quality dissatisfaction
- Missed expectations
- Communication problems
- Lack of responsiveness
- Reduced confidence
- Negative comparisons
- Threats to reduce or stop business

Create:

| Negative Signal | Evidence | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| [Signal] | [Customer statement] | [High/Medium/Low] |

---

# 6️⃣ Identify Neutral Statements

Some customer communication is informational.

Examples:

- "We will send the drawings tomorrow."
- "The meeting is scheduled for Friday."
- "Please send the updated model."

Do not classify neutral statements as negative simply because they are short.

---

# 7️⃣ Identify Critical Signals

Look carefully for statements indicating serious risk.

Examples:

- Customer wants to stop work.
- Customer questions the relationship.
- Customer threatens cancellation.
- Customer requests management intervention.
- Customer says trust has been lost.
- Customer reports repeated unresolved failures.
- Customer indicates they may move work elsewhere.

Critical signals should be escalated according to the company's escalation process.

Do not minimize serious customer concerns.

---

# 8️⃣ Identify Sentiment Drivers

Determine what is causing the sentiment.

Possible drivers:

- Delivery
- Quality
- Communication
- Responsiveness
- Pricing
- Resource availability
- Project management
- Technical issues
- Expectations
- Relationship
- Service experience

Create:

| Sentiment Driver | Evidence | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| [Driver] | [Evidence] | [Impact] |

---

# 9️⃣ Compare With Previous Sentiment

Compare current communication with previous communication when sufficient evidence exists.

Possible changes:

📈 Improving

➡️ Stable

📉 Declining

🔄 Mixed / Changing

⚪ Unknown

Create:

| Period | Sentiment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Previous | [Sentiment] | [Evidence] |
| Current | [Sentiment] | [Evidence] |

Do not claim a trend from a single message.

---

# 🔟 Identify Sentiment Trend

Use available evidence to determine whether sentiment is:

### 🟢 Improving

Positive signals are increasing.

### 🔵 Stable

Sentiment is generally consistent.

### 🟡 Mixed

Positive and negative signals are both present.

### 🟠 Declining

Negative signals are increasing.

### 🔴 Critical

Strong negative signals indicate serious relationship risk.

### ⚪ Unknown

There is insufficient evidence.

---

# 1️⃣1️⃣ Assess Customer Success Risk

Sentiment alone should not determine customer health.

Combine sentiment with:

- Delivery
- Engagement
- Relationship
- Business impact
- Revenue
- Renewal
- Open issues
- Customer feedback

Create:

| Signal | Evidence | Risk Impact |
|---|---|---|
| [Signal] | [Evidence] | [Impact] |

Sentiment is one input into Customer Health, not the entire health score.

---

# 1️⃣2️⃣ Recommend CSM Action

Possible actions:

### Positive Sentiment

- Reinforce successful behavior.
- Capture customer feedback.
- Identify advocacy opportunity.
- Continue normal cadence.

### Neutral Sentiment

- Continue monitoring.
- Ask clarifying questions where useful.
- Maintain normal engagement.

### Negative Sentiment

- Contact customer.
- Understand the concern.
- Create corrective action.
- Monitor closely.

### Critical Sentiment

- Follow escalation process.
- Notify appropriate internal stakeholders.
- Engage management where required.
- Create recovery plan.
- Maintain proactive communication.

Recommendations should be proportional to the evidence.

# 🧠 Sentiment Analysis Example

### Customer Communication

> "The team has been helpful, but we are concerned that the last two deliveries were late."

### Analysis

**Sentiment:** 🟡 Mixed

**Positive Signal:**
Customer recognizes that the team has been helpful.

**Negative Signal:**
Customer is concerned about repeated delivery delays.

**Sentiment Driver:**
Delivery performance.

**Trend:**
Requires comparison with previous communication.

**Recommended Action:**
Review delivery performance and discuss the customer's timeline expectations.

# ⚠️ Important Rules

AI assists — CSM decides.

Do not:

- Guess customer emotions.
- Treat one sentence as the complete customer sentiment.
- Confuse short communication with negative sentiment.
- Invent customer concerns.
- Change health scores based only on AI interpretation.
- Ignore critical customer statements.
- Overreact to minor negative comments.
- Present assumptions as facts.
- Share confidential information unnecessarily.

The most important rule is:

> **Evidence first. Interpretation second. Action third.**
