# ❤️‍🩹 Customer Recovery Plan Workflow

## Purpose

Provide a structured AI-assisted workflow for recovering a customer relationship that has experienced significant problems, dissatisfaction, delivery issues, communication problems, or other risks.

The objective is to move the customer from a difficult situation toward a stable and healthy relationship.

---

# 🔄 Workflow

```text
Customer Problem
      ↓
1. Confirm Recovery Need
      ↓
2. Understand What Happened
      ↓
3. Understand Customer Impact
      ↓
4. Capture Customer Expectations
      ↓
5. Identify Root Cause
      ↓
6. Define Recovery Objectives
      ↓
7. Create Recovery Actions
      ↓
8. Assign Owners
      ↓
9. Set Deadlines
      ↓
10. Communicate Recovery Plan
      ↓
11. Execute Actions
      ↓
12. Monitor Customer Response
      ↓
13. Measure Recovery
      ↓
14. Confirm Recovery
      ↓
15. Return to Normal Cadence
      ↓
16. Document Lessons Learned
```

# 1️⃣ Confirm Recovery Need

Determine whether a formal recovery plan is required.

Possible triggers:

- Serious customer dissatisfaction
- Major delivery failure
- Repeated quality issues
- Significant communication breakdown
- Escalation
- Customer threatens to leave
- Renewal becomes at risk
- Major unresolved issue
- Repeated missed commitments
- Loss of customer trust

Not every small problem requires a formal recovery plan.

Use proportionality.

---

- Customer feedback

# 3️⃣ Understand Customer Impact

Determine how the issue affected the customer.

Possible impacts:

- Project delay
- Additional work
- Increased cost
- Quality concerns
- Missed deadline
- Reduced confidence
- Communication frustration
- Operational disruption
- Business impact
- Relationship damage

Create:

| Issue | Customer Impact | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| [Issue] | [Impact] | [High/Medium/Low] |

Do not exaggerate customer impact.

---

# 4️⃣ Capture Customer Expectations

Identify what the customer wants now.

Possible expectations:

- Immediate resolution
- Clear explanation
- Corrective action
- Better communication
- Faster delivery
- Quality improvement
- Additional support
- Regular progress updates
- Management involvement
- Prevention of recurrence

Separate:

**Customer-Stated Expectations**

from

**CSM Recommendations**

---

# 5️⃣ Identify Root Cause

Use a structured RCA approach.

Possible root-cause categories:

- Process
- People
- Communication
- Requirements
- Resource
- Technology
- Quality control
- Planning
- Customer dependency
- Internal dependency

Ask:

### What happened?

[Description]

### Why did it happen?

[Cause]

### Why was it not detected earlier?

[Detection Gap]

### What allowed it to continue?

[Contributing Factor]

### What should change?

[Corrective Direction]

Do not assign blame without evidence.

Examples:

- Resolve the immediate issue.
- Restore customer confidence.
- Meet revised delivery expectations.
- Improve communication.
- Prevent recurrence.
- Complete outstanding work.
- Rebuild stakeholder relationships.

# 7️⃣ Create Recovery Actions

Create specific corrective actions.

Examples:

- Complete outstanding work.
- Conduct RCA.
- Assign additional resources.
- Establish daily updates.
- Add quality review.
- Schedule management check-in.
- Confirm requirements.
- Create milestone tracking.
- Provide progress report.

| Action | Reason | Expected Outcome |

| [Action] | [Reason] | [Outcome] |

# 8️⃣ Assign Owners

Possible owners:

- CSM
- Delivery Team
- Project Manager
- KAM
- BDE / Sales
- Technical Team
- Finance
- Management

Every important action should have an owner.

If ownership is not confirmed:

**Owner: To Be Confirmed**

Never guess.

# 🔟 Communicate Recovery Plan

Customer communication should include, where appropriate:

### Acknowledgment

Recognize the customer's concern.

### Understanding

Explain what has been understood.

### Action

Explain what is being done.

### Ownership

Clarify who is responsible.

### Timeline

Provide confirmed timelines.

### Follow-Up

Explain when the next update will occur.

Avoid:

- Blaming the customer
- Making excuses
- Overpromising
- Sharing unnecessary internal details
- Providing unconfirmed deadlines

---

🟢 Completed

🔵 In Progress

🟡 Delayed

🔴 Blocked

⚪ Not Started

# 1️⃣2️⃣ Monitor Customer Response

- Delivery performance

- Responsiveness
- Confidence

# 1️⃣4️⃣ Confirm Recovery

Recovery can be considered complete when:

- Immediate issue is resolved.
- Recovery actions are completed.
- Customer impact has been addressed.
- Customer confidence has improved.
- No major unresolved risk remains.
- Preventive actions are established where necessary.

Where appropriate, confirm recovery with the customer.

Do not declare recovery complete based only on internal assumptions.

---

# 1️⃣5️⃣ Return to Normal Cadence

After recovery:

- Return to normal communication cadence.
- Continue monitoring.
- Update customer health.
- Update success plan.
- Maintain stakeholder relationships.
- Review lessons learned.
- Avoid unnecessary over-management.

Recovery should eventually transition back into normal Customer Success management.

# 📊 Recovery Status

Use:

🟢 **Recovered**

Customer issue resolved and relationship is stable.

🟡 **Recovering**

Actions are progressing and customer condition is improving.

🟠 **High Risk**

Recovery actions are underway but significant risk remains.

🔴 **Critical**

Immediate intervention is required.

⚪ **Unknown**

Insufficient information.

# ⚠️ Important Rules

AI assists — CSM decides.

Do not:

- Hide customer-impacting problems.
- Blame the customer without evidence.
- Invent root causes.
- Promise unapproved solutions.
- Give unconfirmed deadlines.
- Declare recovery without evidence.
- Ignore customer feedback.
- Treat internal completion as customer success.
- Escalate unnecessarily.
- Share confidential information unnecessarily.

The goal is:

**Understand → Repair → Restore Trust → Prevent Recurrence**

# 🎯 Desired Outcome

The workflow should help the CSM move from:

**Customer Problem → Understanding → Root Cause → Recovery Actions → Customer Confidence → Stable Relationship**

The goal is not simply to fix the immediate problem.

The goal is to restore a healthy customer relationship and reduce the chance of the same problem happening again.
