# 🤝 Referral Customer Playbook

**Document Type:** Playbook
**Category:** Growth & Expansion
**Owner:** Customer Success Team
**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## When to Use This Playbook

Use this playbook with customers who are genuinely satisfied — 🟢 Green health, positive sentiment expressed unprompted, and ideally past a recent positive milestone (successful renewal, strong QBR, resolved escalation with good outcome). A referral ask outside these conditions tends to feel transactional and can damage trust.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Referral-Ready Customers

- Health status 🟢 Green, sustained for at least one full quarter.
- Look for organic positive signals: unprompted praise, willingness to be a reference, active engagement in QBRs.

## Step 2 — Time the Ask

- Best moments: immediately after a strong QBR, a successful renewal, or the customer volunteering positive feedback.
- Never ask during or immediately after any escalation, even a resolved one — give it distance.

## Step 3 — Make the Ask Directly and Respectfully

- Be specific about what you're asking for (an intro, a case study, a reference call) rather than a vague "let us know if you know anyone."
- Make it easy to say no — this should never feel like pressure.

## Step 4 — Support the Referral

- If they agree, make it as low-effort as possible for them (draft intro text they can forward, brief them on what to expect).
- Keep Sales in the loop on any resulting introduction.

## Step 5 — Close the Loop and Say Thank You

- Regardless of outcome, thank the customer for considering it.
- If the referral converts, follow up with a genuine, specific thank-you — not a generic template.

---

## Do

- Ask only when the relationship is genuinely strong — this playbook is about recognizing the moment, not manufacturing it.
- Make the ask specific and low-effort for the customer.

## Don't

- Don't ask every customer as a matter of routine — an ill-timed ask can undo goodwill.
- Don't follow up repeatedly if the customer doesn't respond — one respectful ask is enough.
