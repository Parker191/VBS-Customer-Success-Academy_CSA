# 💚 What is a Customer Health Score?

## Introduction

A Customer Health Score is a measurable way to understand the overall strength of a customer's relationship with VBS.

Instead of relying on assumptions or intuition, a health score uses data and observations to help Customer Success Managers determine whether a customer is:

- Thriving
- Stable
- At Risk

The health score provides an objective view of customer success and helps CSMs decide where to focus their time and effort.

---

# 🎯 Why Customer Health Scores Matter

Not every customer communicates problems directly.

Some customers gradually reduce engagement, delay responses, postpone projects, or stop sharing future plans.

Without a structured health score, these warning signs can easily be missed.

A Customer Health Score helps VBS:

- Detect risks early.
- Prioritize customer engagement.
- Improve retention.
- Support proactive account management.
- Identify growth opportunities.
- Improve business forecasting.

---

# 🧠 Customer Health vs Customer Happiness

Many new CSMs assume that a happy customer is always a healthy customer.

This is not always true.

| Happy Customer | Healthy Customer |
|---------------|------------------|
| Gives positive feedback | Regularly engages with VBS |
| Enjoys working with the team | Continues investing in projects |
| Appreciates service quality | Responds consistently |
| May still leave unexpectedly | Shows long-term partnership behaviors |

A customer can be happy with the service today but still become unhealthy if communication decreases or future opportunities disappear.

---

# 📊 What Makes a Customer Healthy?

Customer health is influenced by multiple factors working together.

Examples include:

- Communication frequency
- Project activity
- Customer engagement
- Delivery satisfaction
- Payment behavior
- Future opportunities
- Overall relationship strength

No single factor determines customer health.

Instead, Customer Success Managers evaluate the complete picture.

---

# 🏢 How VBS Uses Customer Health Scores

At VBS, Customer Health Scores help different teams make better decisions.

| Team | How Health Scores Help |
|------|------------------------|
| Customer Success | Prioritize customer engagement |
| Sales | Identify expansion opportunities |
| Delivery | Understand customer concerns |
| Finance | Monitor payment-related risks |
| Leadership | Review overall customer portfolio health |

---

# 🔍 Questions Every CSM Should Ask

Before assigning a health status, ask:

- Is the customer responding to communication?
- Are projects progressing smoothly?
- Has the customer provided recent feedback?
- Are invoices being paid on time?
- Is the customer discussing future work?
- Are there unresolved concerns?

The answers help build an objective health assessment.

``` id="healthtimeline"
# 🟢🟡🔴 Understanding the RAG Framework

## Introduction

The RAG Framework is a simple and effective method for monitoring customer health.

RAG stands for:

- 🟢 Green – Healthy
- 🟡 Yellow – Attention Needed
- 🔴 Red – At Risk

Instead of treating every customer the same, the RAG framework helps Customer Success Managers prioritize their time and take the right action based on customer health.

---

# 🎯 Why Use the RAG Framework?

The RAG Framework helps VBS:

- Identify customer risks early.
- Prioritize customer engagement.
- Standardize health reporting.
- Improve cross-functional communication.
- Support proactive account management.

Every customer account should always have a current health status.

---

# 🟢 Green – Healthy Customer

A Green customer has a strong and stable relationship with VBS.

### Typical Characteristics

✅ Regular communication

✅ Positive project experience

✅ Responsive to emails and meetings

✅ Payments are on time

✅ Future opportunities are being discussed

✅ Strong working relationship

### CSM Focus

- Maintain engagement.
- Continue delivering value.
- Explore upsell and cross-sell opportunities.
- Strengthen the relationship.

---

# 🟡 Yellow – Attention Needed

A Yellow customer shows early signs that something may need attention.

The relationship is still recoverable, but the CSM should act before the situation worsens.

### Typical Characteristics

⚠️ Slower communication

⚠️ Delayed project inputs

⚠️ Reduced engagement

⚠️ Minor customer concerns

⚠️ Limited visibility into future work

### CSM Focus

- Increase communication.
- Understand customer concerns.
- Review project status.
- Create a success plan.
- Monitor the account more frequently.

---

# 🔴 Red – At Risk

A Red customer requires immediate attention.

Without timely action, there is a higher risk of losing future business or damaging the relationship.

### Typical Characteristics

🚨 No response from customer

🚨 Major delivery concerns

🚨 Repeated project delays

🚨 Outstanding payment issues

🚨 Negative customer feedback

🚨 Competitor involvement

### CSM Focus

- Escalate internally if required.
- Schedule an urgent customer discussion.
- Create a recovery plan.
- Involve Delivery, Sales, or Leadership when appropriate.
- Track progress closely until the account improves.

---

# 📊 RAG Comparison Table

| Area | 🟢 Green | 🟡 Yellow | 🔴 Red |
|------|-----------|------------|---------|
| Communication | Regular | Slower than usual | No response |
| Customer Sentiment | Positive | Neutral or mixed | Negative |
| Project Progress | On track | Minor delays | Major delays |
| Payment Status | On time | Occasional delays | Overdue or unresolved |
| Future Opportunities | Visible | Uncertain | None identified |
| CSM Priority | Maintain | Monitor | Immediate action |

---

# 🔄 Health Status Can Change

Customer health is not permanent.

A Green customer can become Yellow.

A Yellow customer can recover to Green.

A Red customer can return to Green with proactive management.

### Example Journey

```
January
🟢 Green

February
🟢 Green

March
🟡 Customer responding slowly

April
🟡 Delivery delays identified

May
🔴 Customer escalated concerns

June
🟢 Recovery plan completed successfully
``` id="ragjourney"

This is why regular health reviews are essential.

|--------------|------------------|

| 🔴 Red | Multiple times per week or as required |

# 📊 Health Score Components

## Introduction

A Customer Health Score should be based on measurable indicators rather than assumptions.

At VBS, customer health is evaluated by reviewing several aspects of the customer relationship.

Each area contributes to an overall health score, allowing Customer Success Managers to make objective and consistent decisions.

---

# 🎯 Why Use Multiple Components?

No single indicator tells the complete story.

For example:

- A customer may communicate frequently but have overdue invoices.
- A customer may pay on time but show no interest in future projects.
- A customer may have active projects but provide negative feedback.

Looking at multiple components creates a balanced and reliable health assessment.

# 📈 Customer Engagement Indicators

## Introduction

Customer engagement is one of the strongest predictors of customer health.

Engaged customers communicate regularly, participate in meetings, provide feedback, and actively collaborate with VBS.

Low engagement does not always mean a customer is unhappy, but it should encourage the Customer Success Manager to investigate further.

---

# 🎯 Why Engagement Matters

Engagement shows how connected a customer is to VBS.

Customers who remain engaged are more likely to:

- Continue working with VBS.
- Share future project opportunities.
- Respond to recommendations.
- Build long-term partnerships.
- Provide referrals.

Monitoring engagement helps CSMs identify changes before they become risks.

---

# 📊 Key Customer Engagement Indicators

The following indicators help measure engagement objectively.

| Indicator | Healthy Behaviour |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Email Responses | Responds within a reasonable timeframe |
| Meeting Attendance | Regularly attends scheduled meetings |
| Project Participation | Provides inputs and approvals on time |
| Feedback | Shares constructive feedback |
| Future Discussions | Talks about upcoming projects |
| Relationship Building | Engages with multiple VBS team members |

---

# 🟢 High Engagement

A highly engaged customer typically:

✅ Replies to emails promptly.

✅ Attends review meetings.

✅ Provides project updates.

✅ Responds to questions.

✅ Discusses future work.

✅ Collaborates with VBS teams.

### Example

```

approved project milestones,

``` id="enghigh"
# 🟡 Moderate Engagement

A moderately engaged customer may:

⚠️ Participate only when required.
```

``` id="engmedium"
---

# 🔴 Low Engagement

A low-engagement customer often:

🚨 Does not respond to emails.

🚨 Frequently cancels meetings.

🚨 Stops sharing project information.

🚨 Shows little interest in future discussions.

### Example

```

---

# 📅 Engagement Review Checklist

During every account review, ask:

☐ Has the customer responded recently?

☐ Have meetings been attended?

☐ Are project updates being shared?

☐ Has feedback been received?

☐ Is the customer discussing future plans?

# 🚩 Early Warning Signs

Watch for:

- Longer response times.
- Repeated meeting cancellations.
- Delayed approvals.
- Reduced participation.
- Fewer questions or discussions.

These changes may indicate that customer health is declining.

If engagement decreases:

1. Schedule a relationship check-in.
2. Understand the customer's current priorities.
3. Share relevant insights or value.
4. Introduce new VBS capabilities where appropriate.
5. Maintain a consistent communication cadence.

The goal is to rebuild momentum before the relationship weakens.

# ⚠️ Risk Indicators

## Introduction

Every customer relationship contains some level of risk.

The responsibility of a Customer Success Manager is not to eliminate every risk, but to identify warning signs early and take proactive action.

Risk indicators help VBS protect customer relationships, reduce churn, and improve long-term retention.

---

# 🎯 Why Risk Identification Matters

Most customer relationships do not fail overnight.

Small warning signs usually appear weeks or months before a customer leaves.

Examples include:

- Reduced communication
- Delayed project approvals
- Negative feedback
- Payment delays
- Fewer future project discussions

Recognizing these indicators early gives the CSM an opportunity to recover the relationship.

---

# 📊 Categories of Customer Risk

At VBS, customer risks can be grouped into five categories.

| Risk Category | Examples |
|--------------|----------|
| Communication Risk | Slow responses, missed meetings |
| Delivery Risk | Delays, quality concerns, repeated revisions |
| Financial Risk | Overdue invoices, payment disputes |
| Relationship Risk | Loss of key contacts, reduced engagement |
| Commercial Risk | No future pipeline, competitor activity |

---

# 🗣️ Communication Risk

### Warning Signs

⚠️ Emails go unanswered.

⚠️ Meetings are repeatedly postponed.

⚠️ Response times increase.

⚠️ Customer shares less information.

### CSM Actions

- Schedule a relationship check-in.
- Confirm preferred communication channels.
- Re-engage key stakeholders.
- Record observations in Zoho CRM.

---

# 🏗️ Delivery Risk

### Warning Signs

⚠️ Frequent change requests.

⚠️ Missed milestones.

⚠️ Repeated quality concerns.

⚠️ Escalations from the customer.

### CSM Actions

- Review project status with the Delivery team.
- Understand the root cause.
- Communicate a recovery plan to the customer.
- Monitor progress closely.

---

# 💳 Financial Risk

### Warning Signs

⚠️ Overdue invoices.

⚠️ Delayed payment commitments.

⚠️ Billing disputes.

### CSM Actions

- Coordinate with the Finance team.
- Confirm invoice receipt.
- Understand payment concerns.
- Keep communication professional and solution-focused.

---

# 🤝 Relationship Risk

### Warning Signs

⚠️ Primary contact leaves the company.

⚠️ Executive engagement decreases.

⚠️ Customer participation drops.

⚠️ Feedback becomes limited.

### CSM Actions

- Identify new stakeholders.
- Strengthen relationships across multiple contacts.
- Schedule a business review.
- Rebuild engagement proactively.

---

# 📉 Commercial Risk

### Warning Signs

⚠️ No upcoming projects.

⚠️ Customer stops discussing future plans.

⚠️ Competitors are being evaluated.

⚠️ Reduced project volume.

### CSM Actions

- Explore upcoming business needs.
- Introduce relevant VBS services.
- Demonstrate additional value.
- Share success stories and case studies.

---

# 🚦 Risk Priority Matrix

| Risk Level | Description | Recommended Action |
|------------|-------------|--------------------|
| 🟢 Low | Minor concerns with no immediate impact | Continue monitoring |
| 🟡 Medium | Multiple warning signs appearing | Create a success plan and increase engagement |
| 🔴 High | Significant risk of churn or relationship loss | Immediate action, internal alignment, and recovery plan |

# 🔮 Churn Prediction

## Introduction

Customer churn is the loss of a customer relationship or future business.

In many cases, churn does not happen suddenly.

It is usually the result of a series of small warning signs that appear over time.

A Customer Success Manager should learn to recognize these signals early and take action before the relationship is lost.

---

# 🎯 Why Churn Prediction Matters

Predicting churn allows VBS to:

- Protect customer relationships.
- Improve customer retention.
- Reduce revenue loss.
- Increase customer lifetime value.
- Focus Customer Success efforts where they have the greatest impact.

The earlier a potential churn risk is identified, the greater the chance of recovery.

---

# 📊 Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

Not all customer signals are equally valuable.

### Leading Indicators

These appear **before** churn occurs.

Examples:

- Reduced communication
- Missed review meetings
- Delayed customer approvals
- Lower engagement
- No discussion about future projects
- Declining Health Score

These give the CSM time to act.

---

### Lagging Indicators

These appear **after** the relationship has already weakened.

Examples:

- Customer terminates the partnership
- Projects stop completely
- Competitor wins future work
- Contract is not renewed

By the time lagging indicators appear, recovery is much more difficult.

---

# 🚩 Common Signs of Potential Churn

Watch for patterns such as:

⚠️ Longer response times

⚠️ Repeated meeting cancellations

⚠️ Negative feedback

⚠️ Increased escalations

⚠️ Delayed payments

⚠️ No upcoming project discussions

⚠️ Reduced executive engagement

One sign alone may not indicate churn, but several together should trigger a review.

---

# 📈 Churn Risk Assessment

Use the following guide during account reviews.

| Observation | Risk Level |
|-------------|------------|
| Strong engagement and future opportunities | 🟢 Low |
| Some warning signs but relationship remains active | 🟡 Medium |
| Multiple warning signs with declining engagement | 🔴 High |

---

Evaluate progress.

Questions to ask:

- Has the Health Score increased?
- Are future projects being discussed?
- Has the RAG status changed?

If not, escalate internally and review the long-term account strategy.

# 🚫 Common Mistakes

## Introduction

Customer Health Scores are only effective when they are managed consistently and objectively.

Even experienced Customer Success Managers can make mistakes that reduce the accuracy of health assessments and delay proactive action.

Recognizing these common mistakes helps build better habits and stronger customer relationships.

---

# ❌ Mistake 1 – Marking Every Customer as Green

Some CSMs assume that if there are no complaints, the customer must be healthy.

This can create a false sense of confidence.

### Better Approach

Review communication, engagement, delivery, financial status, and future opportunities before assigning a Green status.

---

# ❌ Mistake 2 – Waiting for the Customer to Complain

Many risks appear long before a customer raises a concern.

Waiting for complaints means reacting too late.

### Better Approach

Monitor trends, identify warning signs, and engage proactively.

---

# ❌ Mistake 3 – Focusing Only on Active Projects

A successful project does not automatically mean the relationship is healthy.

After a project ends, engagement may decrease and future opportunities may disappear.

### Better Approach

Continue relationship-building after project completion through regular check-ins and business reviews.

---

# ❌ Mistake 4 – Ignoring Declining Engagement

Slower responses, cancelled meetings, or reduced communication are often early warning signs.

Ignoring these signals increases the risk of churn.

### Better Approach

Review the Health Score and reconnect with the customer before the relationship weakens further.

---

# ❌ Mistake 5 – Forgetting to Update Zoho CRM

A Health Score without supporting CRM records creates confusion for other teams.

Missing notes also make future account reviews less effective.

### Better Approach

Update meeting summaries, customer notes, action items, and Health Score changes immediately after meaningful interactions.

---

# ❌ Mistake 6 – Using Personal Opinions Instead of Evidence

Statements such as:

> "I think they're happy."

or

> "They've always been a good customer."

are not enough.

### Better Approach

Use measurable indicators such as:

- Communication history
- Project performance
- Customer feedback
- Payment behaviour
- Growth discussions

---

# ❌ Mistake 7 – Treating Health Scores as Permanent

Customer health changes over time.

A Green account today may become Yellow next month if engagement declines.

Likewise, a Red account can recover with the right action plan.

### Better Approach

Review and update Health Scores regularly.

---

| Mistake | Better Practice |
|----------|-----------------|
| Marking every account Green | Use objective evidence |
| Waiting for complaints | Be proactive |
| Looking only at projects | Review the entire relationship |
| Ignoring engagement | Monitor trends consistently |
| Skipping CRM updates | Keep records current |
| Relying on opinions | Use measurable indicators |
| Never updating Health Scores | Review them regularly |
