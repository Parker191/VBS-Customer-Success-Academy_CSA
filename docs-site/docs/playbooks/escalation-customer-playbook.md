# 🚨 Escalation Customer Playbook

**Document Type:** Playbook
**Category:** Escalation Management
**Owner:** Customer Success Team
**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## When to Use This Playbook

Use this playbook whenever a customer raises a formal complaint, threatens to churn, requests to speak with leadership, or when an issue has repeated despite prior attempts to resolve it. This is distinct from the [At-Risk Customer Playbook](at-risk-customer-playbook.md) — an escalation is an active, urgent event, not a gradual health decline.

**Common trigger signals:**
- Customer explicitly says they are unhappy, frustrated, or considering leaving
- Request to speak with a manager or executive
- Same issue reported more than once without resolution
- Missed commitment or deadline that materially affected the customer
- Legal, compliance, or contractual dispute raised
- Public complaint (social media, review site, referenceable channel)

---

## Step 1 — Acknowledge Immediately (Within 2 Hours)

- Respond to the customer within 2 business hours of the escalation being raised, even if you don't yet have a resolution.
- Use the Escalation Acknowledgement Email template as a starting point, personalized to the specific issue.
- Do not attempt to resolve the issue in the acknowledgement message — the goal here is to confirm it's been received and is being taken seriously.

## Step 2 — Classify Severity (Within 4 Hours)

| Severity | Definition | Response Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Critical | Contract/revenue at risk, legal exposure, or executive-level complaint | CSM + Manager + relevant department head |
| 🟠 High | Repeated unresolved issue, strong dissatisfaction | CSM + Manager |
| 🟡 Moderate | Single unresolved issue, customer frustrated but not threatening to leave | CSM, manager informed |

## Step 3 — Internal Alignment (Same Day)

- Loop in the relevant internal owner (Delivery, Product, Finance, Legal) based on root cause.
- Agree internally on the resolution options and any limits (commercial concessions, timeline commitments) before speaking to the customer again.
- For 🔴 Critical severity, ensure your manager is briefed before any further customer communication.

## Step 4 — Resolve & Communicate (Timeline depends on severity)

- Present the customer with a clear plan: what will be done, by whom, and by when.
- Avoid vague reassurance ("we're looking into it") without a concrete next update time.
- Set a specific follow-up date and keep it, even if the update is "still in progress."

## Step 5 — Close & Document

- Confirm resolution directly with the customer — don't assume silence means satisfaction.
- Log the full escalation timeline in CRM: trigger, severity, actions taken, resolution, and time-to-resolve.
- Conduct an internal root-cause review for 🔴 Critical and repeated 🟠 High escalations to prevent recurrence.

---

## Do

- Acknowledge fast, even before you have a solution — silence is what turns frustration into churn risk.
- Give specific dates, not vague timelines.
- Involve your manager early for anything 🟠 High or above — don't wait until it's unmanageable.

## Don't

- Don't promise a fix or timeline you haven't confirmed internally.
- Don't let an escalation go quiet after the first response — follow up even with partial progress.
- Don't skip the internal root-cause review — repeated escalations from the same cause are a signal of a systemic issue, not a customer issue.
