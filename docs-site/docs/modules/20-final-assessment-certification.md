Question 1 — Customer Success Fundamentals

What is the primary goal of Customer Success?

A. Resolve only customer complaints.

B. Ensure customers achieve desired outcomes and receive value.

C. Increase internal meetings.

D. Manage only invoices.

Correct Answer: B

Question 2 — Customer Health

What is the purpose of a Customer Health Score?

A. Measure employee performance.

B. Identify customer status, risks, and opportunities.

C. Track marketing campaigns.

D. Replace customer conversations.

Correct Answer: B

Question 3 — Renewal Management

When should a CSM start renewal conversations?

A. Only after the contract expires.

B. Near the final day.

C. Throughout the customer lifecycle.

D. Only when Sales asks.

Correct Answer: C

Question 4 — Customer Playbooks

What is the purpose of a customer playbook?

A. Provide guidance for repeatable customer situations.

B. Store invoices.

C. Replace CRM.

D. Track attendance.

Correct Answer: A

Question 5 — SOPs

Why are SOPs important?

A. They create consistent execution.

B. They eliminate customer interaction.

C. They replace CSM judgment.

D. They are only for new employees.

Correct Answer: A

Question 6 — At-Risk Customers

What should a CSM do when a customer becomes at risk?

A. Wait until the customer complains.

B. Ignore the risk until renewal.

C. Identify the root cause and create a recovery plan.

D. Immediately transfer the account to Sales.

Correct Answer: C

Question 7 — Customer Onboarding

What is the best approach to customer onboarding?

A. Send documents and wait.

B. Define goals, expectations, milestones, and responsibilities.

C. Discuss renewal immediately.

D. Only introduce the project team.

Correct Answer: B

Question 8 — Adoption

Which activity best supports customer adoption?

A. Regularly connecting product or service usage to customer goals.

B. Sending invoices.

C. Reducing customer communication.

D. Waiting for complaints.

Correct Answer: A

Question 9 — QBR / Business Review

What is an important purpose of a QBR or business review?

A. Discuss only outstanding invoices.

B. Review value, outcomes, risks, priorities, and next steps.

C. Replace all operational meetings.

D. Sell unrelated services.

Correct Answer: B

Question 10 — CRM Documentation

What should a CRM customer note contain?

A. Only the customer's name.

B. Only the invoice number.

C. Relevant context, sentiment, risks, actions, and follow-up.

D. Personal opinions unrelated to the account.

Correct Answer: C

Question 11 — Escalation Management

What is the best response to a customer escalation?

A. Defend the internal team immediately.

C. Ignore it until the next meeting.

D. Tell the customer to contact another department.

Correct Answer: B

Question 12 — Proactive Customer Success

What does proactive Customer Success mean?

A. Waiting for customers to request help.

🎯 Practical Case Study Assessment
Customer Scenario: Architecture Firm Account Recovery
Background

You are the Customer Success Manager for a VBS customer.

The customer has been working with VBS for 8 months for BIM modeling services.

The relationship has historically been active, but recent customer engagement and project activity indicate potential account risk.

Customer Situation

Recently, you observe:

Customer project activity has reduced.
No new project request has been received for 45 days.
Customer has not attended the last scheduled review meeting.
One previous delivery received delayed feedback.
Invoice payment is pending beyond the due date.

These signals suggest that the account may require immediate Customer Success intervention.

Task 1 — Customer Health Assessment

Evaluate the current customer health status.

Choose one:

🟢 Healthy

🟡 Needs Attention

🔴 At Risk

Explain your decision.

Your response should consider:

Customer engagement.
Project activity.
Delivery experience.
Customer communication.
Financial status.
Retention risk.
Task 2 — Identify Customer Risks

Identify the risks associated with the account.

Relationship Risks

Consider:

Reduced communication.
Missed customer meetings.
Reduced engagement.
Changes in stakeholder involvement.
Delivery Risks

Consider:

Delayed feedback.
Potential delivery dissatisfaction.
Service quality concerns.
Unresolved operational issues.
Financial Risks

Consider:

Overdue invoice.
Payment delays.
Potential billing disputes.
Need for Finance coordination.
Retention Risks

Consider:

Reduced project activity.
Lack of new requirements.
Customer disengagement.
Potential competitor evaluation.
Reduced perceived value.
Task 3 — Create an Action Plan

Create a structured recovery plan.

Action	Owner	Timeline
Customer discussion	CSM	Within 1 week
Delivery review	Delivery Team	Within 3 days
Payment follow-up	Finance + CSM	Immediate
Customer health review	CSM	Within 3 days
Recovery plan	CSM	Within 2 weeks
Follow-up review	CSM + Customer	Within 2 weeks

The action plan should clearly define:

What needs to happen.
Who owns the action.
When it must happen.
What outcome is expected.
Task 4 — Customer Communication

Draft a professional customer email.

The communication should:

Acknowledge the existing relationship.
Recognize the customer's current situation.
Request a discussion.
Demonstrate willingness to support.
Focus on customer needs.
Avoid sounding like a sales follow-up.
Avoid unnecessary pressure.
Provide a clear next step.
Example Communication Framework

Your email should generally follow this structure:

Professional greeting.
Acknowledge the relationship.
Mention the recent change in activity or engagement.
Express concern and willingness to understand.
Request a short discussion.
Offer support.
Close professionally.
Task 5 — CRM Update

Create a CRM note that includes:

Situation Summary

Document the current customer situation.

Customer Sentiment

Document the known or observed customer sentiment.

Risks

Document:

Relationship risks.
Delivery risks.
Financial risks.
Retention risks.
Actions Planned

Document the actions agreed or planned.

Follow-up Date

Document the next follow-up date.

🎭 CSM Simulation Exercise
Scenario

A customer contacts you and says:

"We are not happy with the recent delivery timeline and are considering other options."

This is a potential escalation and retention risk.

Your responsibility is to respond as a Customer Success Manager.

Step 1 — Immediate Response

Explain how you would acknowledge the customer's concern.

Your response should demonstrate:

Empathy.
Ownership.
Professionalism.
Active listening.
Commitment to understanding the issue.

Avoid:

Blaming the delivery team.
Becoming defensive.
Immediately offering a discount.
Making promises before investigating.
Step 2 — Investigation

Identify the information you need.

Consider:

Project details.
Expected delivery timeline.
Actual delivery timeline.
Customer expectations.
Scope and requirements.
Changes in requirements.
Internal constraints.
Communication history.
Previous delivery performance.
Impact on the customer.
Step 3 — Internal Coordination

Explain who you would involve.

Potential stakeholders include:

Delivery Manager.
Project Manager.
Project Team.
Production Team.
Customer Success Leadership.
Sales / KAM when appropriate.
Finance when financial matters are involved.

The CSM should coordinate internally while maintaining clear ownership of the customer relationship.

Step 4 — Recovery Plan

Create a recovery plan that includes:

Immediate Actions

Identify what must happen immediately.

Timeline

Define when each action will be completed.

Customer Communication

Define:

Who will communicate.
What will be communicated.
When communication will occur.
How progress will be shared.
Prevention Steps

Identify actions to prevent the same issue from recurring.

Examples may include:

Improved project planning.
Clearer expectations.
Earlier risk escalation.
More frequent progress updates.
Improved internal coordination.
Updated SOPs.
Improved quality or delivery checkpoints.
Step 5 — Follow-up

Explain how you would confirm customer satisfaction after the issue is resolved.

A strong follow-up should:

Confirm the issue has been resolved.
Ask whether expectations are now being met.
Confirm customer confidence.
Review remaining concerns.
Document the outcome in CRM.
Monitor the account after resolution.
