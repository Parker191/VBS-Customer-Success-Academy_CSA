# 👋 Welcome

Reporting is a critical skill for modern Customer Success Managers.

Customer Success teams collect large amounts of information:

- Customer interactions
- Project performance
- Health scores
- Revenue data
- Customer feedback
- Risks
- Growth opportunities

The ability to organize and communicate this information helps teams make better decisions.

This module teaches how to convert customer data into meaningful business insights.

---

After completing this module, you will be able to:

- Understand the purpose of Customer Success reporting.
- Identify important Customer Success KPIs.
- Build customer health dashboards.
- Create executive-level reports.
- Track customer activity and engagement.
- Communicate risks and opportunities.
- Use AI to improve reporting efficiency.

# 📊 Importance of Reporting

Customer Success reporting helps teams understand what is happening across customer accounts and identify where action is required.

A good report does not simply show data.

It answers important business questions:

- Are customers healthy?
- Are customers receiving value?
- Are risks increasing?
- Are accounts growing?
- Where should the team focus attention?

---

# 💼 Why Reporting Matters

## For Customer Success Managers

Reporting helps CSMs:

- Prioritize accounts.
- Identify risks early.
- Track customer engagement.
- Prepare for customer meetings.
- Document progress.

---

## For Leadership

Reporting helps leaders:

- Understand customer health.
- Forecast revenue.
- Identify business risks.
- Measure team performance.
- Make strategic decisions.

---

## Customer Success Reporting Principle

> **"A dashboard should drive action, not just display information."**

# 📊 Dashboard Fundamentals

A Customer Success dashboard is a visual summary of important account information.

A good dashboard allows teams to quickly understand performance, risks, and opportunities.

---

# Elements of an Effective Dashboard

## 1. Clear Objective

Every dashboard should answer a specific question.

Example:

"Which customers need immediate attention?"

---

## 2. Relevant Metrics

Include only information that supports decisions.

Avoid adding unnecessary data.

---

## 3. Visual Indicators

Use simple indicators:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 🟢 Green | Healthy |
| 🟡 Yellow | Needs attention |
| 🔴 Red | High risk |

---

## 4. Action-Oriented Insights

A dashboard should show:

- What happened?
- Why did it happen?
- What action is required?

---

# Dashboard Structure Example

```text
Customer Portfolio Overview

Total Accounts
       │
       ▼
Health Distribution
       │
       ▼
At-Risk Customers
       │
       ▼
Required Actions
```

# Example Activity Dashboard

# Important Note

Activity metrics should not be measured only by volume.

A CSM having 20 meetings is not automatically successful.

The focus should be:

**Quality of engagement + Customer outcomes**

# 👔 Executive Reporting

Executive reports provide leadership with a high-level view of customer performance, risks, opportunities, and business impact.

Executives usually need insights—not detailed activity logs.

---

# Executive Report Structure

## 1. Portfolio Overview

Include:

- Total customers managed.
- Healthy accounts.
- At-risk accounts.
- Revenue overview.

---

## 2. Key Achievements

Examples:

- Successful customer onboarding.
- Customer expansion.
- Improved engagement.
- Resolved escalations.

---

## 3. Risks

Examples:

- Churn risks.
- Payment delays.
- Delivery concerns.
- Reduced engagement.

---

## 4. Opportunities

Examples:

- Upsell opportunities.
- Cross-sell opportunities.
- Customer referrals.

---

## 5. Recommended Actions

Clearly mention:

- What needs attention?
- Who owns the action?
- Expected timeline.

---

# Executive Summary Example

```
Portfolio Health: 85% Green

Highlights:
- Two customers expanded services.
- Three accounts completed successful reviews.

Risks:
- One customer showing reduced engagement.

Next Actions:
- Schedule recovery meeting.
- Review expansion opportunities.
```

# ⭐ Reporting Best Practices

Effective reporting is not about creating more reports.

It is about creating useful insights that help teams make better decisions.

---

## Best Practices

### 1. Focus on Business Outcomes

Reports should answer:

- Are customers successful?
- Are risks increasing?
- Are opportunities available?
- What action is required?

---

### 2. Keep Reports Simple

Avoid:

- Too many unnecessary metrics.
- Complex dashboards.
- Data without context.

Focus on information that drives decisions.

---

### 3. Maintain Data Accuracy

Ensure:

- CRM records are updated.
- Customer information is current.
- Metrics are calculated consistently.
- Ownership is clearly defined.

---

### 4. Add Insights, Not Just Numbers

Example:

❌ "Customer engagement decreased by 30%."

✅ "Customer engagement decreased by 30% due to reduced project activity. A proactive check-in is scheduled to understand future requirements."

---

> 💡 **Remember**

Numbers explain what happened.

Insights explain what to do next.

---

---

# ⚠️ Common Reporting Mistakes

Avoid these common reporting problems:

---

## 1. Tracking Too Many Metrics

More data does not always mean better decisions.

Focus on meaningful KPIs.

---

## 2. Reporting Without Action

A dashboard should always answer:

"What should happen next?"

---

## 3. Using Outdated Information

Old CRM data can create incorrect decisions.

Update information regularly.

---

## 4. Ignoring Customer Context

Numbers alone do not explain customer situations.

Combine data with customer conversations.

---

## 5. Creating Reports Only for Management

Reports should help CSMs manage accounts better—not only update leadership.

---

> ✅ **Key Takeaway**

A great report creates clarity, alignment, and action.
